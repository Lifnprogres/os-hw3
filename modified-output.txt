Experiment 1: two threads take two locks in opposite orders
  T1 holds the lock of account 0 and waits for the lock of account 1
  T2 holds the lock of account 1 and waits for the lock of account 0
  deadlock detected: T1 -> T2 -> T1 (each thread waits for a lock the next one holds)
  recovery: T2 is chosen as the victim (it moves less money, so it is the cheaper one to redo)
  T1: moved 100 from account 0 to account 1
  T2: rolled back: released account 1, moved nothing
  balances [400, 600, 500], total 1500

  Prevention: every thread locks the lower-numbered account first (three threads in a ring)
  lock-order check: PASS (a transfer from 2 to 0 locks account 0 first, then account 2)
  60,000 transfers finished, balances [500, 500, 500], total 1500 (started at 1500)

Experiment 2: the banker's algorithm (resource types A, B, C)
  total [9, 5, 6], free [3, 1, 4]
  starting state: safe, order P2 P1 P3 P4
  P3 asks for [2, 0, 2]: granted; a safe order is P2 P3 P1 P4
